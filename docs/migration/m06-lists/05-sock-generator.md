# M0.6 piece 5: The Sock generator (behaviour list)

Words follow `GLOSSARY.md`. Paths are in `reference/it-socks/` (tag v0.1.1) unless they start with `game/`, `docs/` or
`data/` of the port (a line says "port" then). "Old" is the game at tag v0.1.1. A line is one thing the designer can keep or
drop. Nothing is left out as minor.

The old generator is **`SockLayout`** (`game/world/sock_layout.gd`, 1726 lines): "every Sock's resting pose computed from the
game's seed, with no physics drop and no pre-baked file" (sock_layout.gd:1-23). Its numbers are in
`data/tuning/mess.json` "spill" and "mates_*", `data/tuning/stages.json` (stage Pairs, high spots) and `data/tuning/grotto.json`.
Its places come from the house's data file `assets/house/house_layout.json` read by `FlatLayout` (`game/house/flat_layout.gd`)
and the furniture tops found on the meshes (`HouseParts.tops()`, `game/house/house_parts.gd:241-360`). It is not under the names
spawn_socks / scatter / fill_floor: it is `bake()` (a New Game) and `spawn()` (a floor's stage). `SockPour`
(`game/world/sock_pour.gd`) is the old zoo's pour, ported already as the port's heap spout.

Scope tags used below:
- **(zoo)** can exist in a zoo station today.
- **(needs Stages)** the old floor-by-floor campaign (M4 in the port, Q2385).
- **(needs the washing machine)** the old basement machine; the port has none yet.
- **(needs the Grotto)** the old Lost Sock Grotto level (switched off in the port until ported, Q2144).
- **(needs a house)** needs the house built from the kit with rooms, stairs and levels (house/03 on).

## Behaviour list

### A. When it runs and who runs it

1. When a New Game starts, then the Host lays the whole starting Mess from the game's seed before anyone joins, behind the loading screen "The washer bursts open...", with every player frozen. (needs a house) (changed by Q2518-Q2524: the port's generator lays a level's starting Mess from its SockAreas when the level starts, on every machine before anything binds, and a Guest's snapshot replaces it with the Host's; no loading screen) (ref: game/core/main.gd:637-666)
2. When guests are connected at a New Game's bake, then it refuses ("a New Game's layout never runs while guests are connected"); guests waiting in the Lobby join once it is laid. (co-op) (ref: game/core/main.gd:640-645)
3. When a floor opens (its Fix, the campaign on), then the Host lays that stage's Socks the same way onto the house as it stands and onto the Socks already lying there, and lands them for everyone. (needs Stages) (Stages are M4; the generator's `records()` is what a stage's spawn will call) (ref: game/campaign/campaign_installer.gd:13-18, 40-50; game/world/sock_layout.gd:299-360)
4. When a stage's Socks come due while one stage's spawn is still being laid, then the next waits and runs right after (the zoo's quick stages). (needs Stages) (ref: game/campaign/campaign_installer.gd:46, 68)
5. When the drum's own stage is the current one, then the generator does not run: the drum's Lost Socks are laid by the drum's rules. (needs the washing machine) (ref: game/campaign/campaign_installer.gd:46-47; game/campaign/campaign.gd:205-206)
6. When the layout runs, then its plan (where every slot is and which Design takes it) runs on a worker thread and the laying runs a slice a frame within a frame budget, so the loading screen keeps animating and a stage's spawn never hitches the Fix's frame. (the port lays a station's 110 Socks in the level's start-up frame; no worker thread or slicing yet: a [later] for a floor of about 1200) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:247-265, 318-348, 363-365; game/core/main.gd:346)
7. When the progress is asked, then it is 0.1 once planned, then 0.1 + 0.9 x the share of Socks laid (the loading bar). (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:280-287)
8. When the same seed, Designs, levels and high share were laid before in this process, then the stored rows are returned at once, not laid again (a memo). (not ported: no memo) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:233-238, 276)
9. When the layout is done, then it prints one line: Socks, ms (planning, laying), how many moved to open floor, how many forced, tries a Sock. (the port keeps the report as `SockGenerator.last_report`: asked, laid, left out, the fails by kind and reason, the hidden Socks moved to open floor; a warning in the log when a Sock finds no place) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:273-275)

12. When a stage spawns, then it spawns at least what each unlocked Cabinet's unlocked Rows still need, then more of their Designs up to "stages.pairs" for that stage (50, 160, 150, 130, 50: basement, ground, first, attic, grotto), scaled down when Drawers are smaller than full. (needs Stages) (changed by Q2520; the stage numbers wait for Stages, M4) (ref: game/campaign/campaign.gd:196-249; data/tuning/stages.json pairs)

(changed by Q2520: no scaling; each area has its count) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:1114-1121)

16. When the planned slots add up to more than the Socks, then the last planned ones are cut (the slot list is cut to the Sock count). (changed by Q2520: never cut; a Sock that finds no place is left out and named in the log) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:1199)

### C. Which Designs, and in what share

### E. Where Socks may not land (exclusions and spacing)

36. When a floor point is drawn, then it is on a 0.2 m grid, at a random point of its cell, in a room that is not a stair zone, 0.28 m in from the room's walls (0.18 + half a cell). (needs a house) (changed by Q2527: `wall_margin` 0.28 m on the area's SockAreaRules; open floor is a surface within `floor_band` of the area's bottom face) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:84-85, 1495-1541)
37. When a floor point lies in a stairwell's hole (a flight from below opens into the room), then it is not open floor (the hole grown 0.2 m). (needs a house) (changed by Q2527: a hole's floor lies outside the band) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:1520-1532)
38. When a point lies in a clear zone (in front of every Cabinet, round the Wardrobe Station and the washer's, grown 0.1 m, up to their y_max), then no floor Sock lies there, and no other Sock either (the check on every try). (zoo) (changed by Q2527: the box in front of every Cabinet, `cabinet_clear` 1.8 x 0.95 m, and SockExclusion boxes) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:543-551, 1544-1563; game/house/flat_layout.gd:527-541)
39. When a point lies on a furniture footprint (grown 5 cm, within 0.3 m of its base height), then it is not open floor. (zoo) (changed by Q2527: `furniture_margin` 0.05 m) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:1547-1567)
40. When a point in the attic is under a roof lower than 1 m over the floor (where a crouching player does not fit), then it is not open floor. (needs a house) (changed by Q2527: `headroom` 1 m over open floor) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:1572-1573)
41. When floor points are drawn, then each is weighted by how thickly Socks lie there: exp(-walk / "gradient_falloff" (5 m)), never under "gradient_floor" (0.2), by the walking distance from the washer, so the Mess thins out away from the machine. A Sock sent to open floor after failing (row 52) is drawn evenly instead. (needs the washing machine) (kept by Q2528, from a washer marker, in a straight line until the house is walked; only open floor and tops) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:1483-1493, 1534-1535, 960-976; data/tuning/mess.json gradient_falloff, gradient_floor)
42. When a try's point is not in a room or has left the house (lost), then it fails. (zoo) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:543-547)
43. When a floor Sock's try lands on furniture (more than 0.25 m over the floor it was planned on), then it fails. (zoo) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:540-542)
44. When a Sock's two ends would lie more than 3 cm higher or lower than its middle (over a top's edge, a step), then it would hang: the try fails. (zoo) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:57-58, 536-539)
45. When a Sock inside an openable would poke out of its floor plan (turned box), then the try fails. (zoo) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:517-522)
46. When a Sock on a top lies shut in (every look up, level and 45 degrees up round the compass meets the house within 1.2 m: a piano's keys under its lid), then the try fails (house/22). (zoo) (not ported: the port's tops are already cut to 12 cm headroom) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:45-48, 605-616, 756-759)
47. When a Sock would rest on a ledge (a leg's foot, a rim: held up more than 1 cm over what the rays found, or more than 3 cm over the floor it was planned on; 1 cm on a top), then the try fails. (zoo) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:40-44, 735-744)
48. When a Sock would stand over its space's ceiling (a shelf, a crawl space's top, 1.2 m over a floor), then the try fails and its space counts as full. (zoo) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:745-747)
49. When a Sock would start inside the house or a laid Sock even raised 3 x 3 cm, or would fall more than 1.5 m (a void), or is not touching what holds it (1 cm under), then the try fails. (zoo) (changed: the port tries rays and an overlap test, no cast) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:34-39, 716-755)
50. When Socks are laid, then Socks never overlap each other: each next Sock comes down onto the laid ones' boxes (row 55). There is no other spacing rule between Socks (no minimum distance). (zoo) (changed by Q2526: a flat area keeps `spacing` (0.02 m) between Socks) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:15-23, 794-860)
51. When a new spawn is laid onto a Mess that already lies, then the lying Socks are marked first, so new ones come down on top of them, never inside. (needs Stages) (the port avoids every lying Sock of the Mess) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:336-344, 913-926)
52. When a slot fails all its tries (8; an inside slot's first go 6 x 8 = 48, as a Car's cabin is mostly seats), then it moves to open floor of its level, evenly drawn, at most 6 times, 8 tries each; a space found full to its top 3 times in a slot, or 4 slots in all, takes no more. (zoo) (changed by Q2521: a Sock tries other places of its own sort, hidden or open, `repicks` times before the open floor) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:49-67, 376-421, 960-976)
53. When a Sock has nowhere left, then it is forced: laid on its last spot whatever the house says, flat, turned at random, 2 mm over the floor. (zoo) (changed: never forced; it is left out and named in the log) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:405-409, 465-467)
54. When the layout runs, then every room Door is open and every hatch shut; a door or drawer that moved, and the Radio, are put where they stand first. (needs a house) (the port lays onto the house as the level stands) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:188-218; game/core/main.gd:661-662)

### F. How a Sock comes to rest (computed, no physics step)

55. When a Sock is laid, then it is turned at random about up, its lean comes from what lies under its two ends (the house by rays and the Socks laid before by a 4 cm height map), at most a slope of 0.4 (about 22 degrees), and it comes straight down onto the house and the laid Socks as its collider boxes (a shape cast), resting 0.5 mm over what it meets. It never stands on its cuff or toe. (zoo) (changed: rays at its middle and ends and an overlap test, flat (a trail's leaned with the ramp), no lean from what lies under it; the rest computed the same in the editor and the game) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:15-23, 30-39, 641-766, 901-903)
56. When a floor Sock is laid on a Heap, then it first rolls down the Heap where it is steeper than 0.4 (steps of 0.12 m, at most 12, never into a wall or furniture), so a Heap slumps into a mound instead of a column. (zoo) (changed by Q2526: only in a "heap" area) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:68-75, 523-526, 557-594)
57. When a Heap is boxed in by walls and furniture and can not slump, then it grows at most 0.4 m over its floor; then it is full and its next Socks go elsewhere. (zoo) (changed by Q2526) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:75-76, 705-710)
58. When a floor Sock's later tries are drawn, then they lie within a normal spread of 7 cm of its planned point (a Heap's, or the first 3 tries), else 4 cm x the try number. (zoo) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:60-61, 511-513)
59. When the Socks are laid, then rarer Designs go first (a random key times "mess.hiding_weight" by Rarity: common 1, uncommon 1.5, rare 3), so they lie deeper in the Heaps (Q839); the rows keep the plan's order. (zoo) (dropped by Q2521: rarity no longer decides hiding) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:376-417; data/tuning/mess.json hiding_weight)
60. When the layout is done, then every Sock is Resting (a still body, drawn in bulk); none is moving. (zoo) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:3-5; docs/adr/0008-two-tier-socks.md)

### G. Mates: where a Sock's mate lies

61. When Designs take their slots, then a Design's two Socks are put a Mate distance apart, walked through the house (Q409): from "mate_distance_min" (0.4 m) for the Design found first to "mate_distance_max" (6 m) for the one found last (a shuffled order, commons first), times the Rarity's factor (common 1.0, uncommon 1.4, rare 2.0), capped at 8 m. (zoo) (changed by Q2525: straight line, 0.4 to 6 m, no Rarity factor) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:1281-1305; data/tuning/mess.json mate_distance_*, rarity_distance_factor)
62. When the mate's slot is sought, then up to 96 free slots are tried and the one whose walk is nearest the Mate distance wins; within 0.25 m it is taken at once. The Pairs with the longest distance are placed first, while every slot is still free. (zoo) (kept, straight line) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:81-82, 1307-1365)
63. When a slot is picked for a Design, then it is a hiding spot (inside an openable, under furniture, on a top, or in a Heap) or an open one, the hiding spots weighted by the Rarity's hiding weight against the open slots: rarer Designs hide more often. Never told to the player (Q839). (zoo) (dropped by Q2521: every Design hides alike; the hidden share is one number on the area, 0.3) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:1323-1327, 1394-1411)
64. When "mess.mates_across_levels.share" names a Rarity (rare 0.5; common and uncommon 0), then that share of its Pairs has the mate on another level, at most 1 level up or down, as near its Mate distance as that level allows (Q446); those are placed first. (needs a house) (parked by Q2528: one area per floor today; levels come with the house) (ref: game/world/sock_layout.gd:1306-1310, 1330-1345, 1420-1438; data/tuning/mess.json mates_across_levels)

### H. The seed and determinism

65. When a New Game starts, then it picks a seed of its own at random and saves it with the game; with random layouts off (the tests, the shots, the zoo) it uses "mess.seed" (1). (changed by Q2524: the level's seed by default; `new_seed_each_start` picks one each start, kept in GeneratedSocks' state, so a snapshot and a save carry it) (ref: game/core/game.gd:283, 808-813; data/tuning/mess.json seed)
66. When a stage spawns, then its seed is the game's seed hashed with the number of unlocked floors. (needs Stages) (Stages, M4) (ref: game/core/game.gd:816-818; game/campaign/campaign_installer.gd:49-50)

## Findings for the rework

What was weak or tangled in the old generator:

1. **Data lived in four places, none of them in the editor.** Counts in `mess.json` keyed by node names ("Fridge", "UnderBed"), places in `house_layout.json` (rooms, clear zones, crawl spaces, openables), tops computed at load from a 1.16 MB manifest of mesh cells (`house_parts.gd:241`), stage numbers in `stages.json`. Renaming a piece silently dropped its Socks (`.get(o["name"], 0)`, sock_layout.gd:1133). A level designer could not see any of it. (changed by Q2518-Q2524: the port's generator lays a level's starting Mess from its SockAreas when the level starts, on every machine before anything binds, and a Guest's snapshot replaces it with the Host's; no loading screen)
2. **Everything generated, nothing authored.** The only hand-placed Socks were the washer lesson, patched over the result afterwards (`with_washer_lesson`, sock_layout.gd:1675). There was no way to say "three Socks on this shelf, here".
3. **One RNG stream for the whole house.** Every slot, try, Heap and Mate draws from one `_rng`; a change anywhere shifts every Sock after it. The code says so twice: a layout change left the Car empty "by luck of the seed" (sock_layout.gd:53-55), and a prop's wider clear ring "moved the seeded bakes for nothing" (house_parts.gd:266). Determinism held only for identical inputs. (Stages are M4; the generator's `records()` is what a stage's spawn will call)
4. **Determinism also depended on the world's state**: door poses, the Radio, the lying Socks, the physics engine (rows 51, 54, 68). Fine for the Host-computes rule, fragile for a preview that must match the game.
5. **Cost.** A hidden Jolt space for the house plus tile spaces for laid Socks (about 245 a bake, a pool kept for the process because Jolt never gives memory back, sock_layout.gd:96-99), a worker thread for the plan, frame slicing, a memo; a stage spawn still hitched the Fix 100-290 ms (feature catalogue P60). 1210 Socks took about 1 s headless (ADR-0005).
6. **Tangled concerns in one 1726-line node**: Design choice, counts, places, Mate distance by walking, hiding odds, rest physics, Heaps, stairs, the Grotto, the lesson, reporting. Mate distance needs the walk graph of a whole house, which the port does not have. (the port lays a station's 110 Socks in the level's start-up frame; no worker thread or slicing yet: a [later] for a floor of about 1200)
7. **Two rest paths.** The layout computed poses by its own cast-and-heightmap physics, different from how the Mess's bodies really settle; a Sock laid by one could wake and move under the other.
8. **Good ideas worth keeping**: Host computes, Guests take rows; rejection rules per try (edge, ledge, shut in, ceiling); under-furniture edge strips so the crosshair reaches; rarer laid first (deeper); a report of moved and forced Socks; never-stepped space so frame timing does not matter. (not ported: no memo)

## Questions for the designer (answered)

1. **What an area is.** (a) Boxes only: a `SockArea` box volume, Socks land on whatever surface is under it inside the box (floor, a top, a shelf), found by a ray down. (b) A floor polygon drawn on the floor (Path3D or polygon points) plus boxes for raised places. (c) Boxes plus furniture-top markers: the furniture's own `Spots/Top<n>` count as areas. Recommended: (a) with (c): one box node covers floors, Heaps and shelves with one rule, the editor's box gizmo is built in, and the tops the furniture already carries become areas without a second shape type; a polygon adds a gizmo to write for little gain in rooms that are mostly rectangles. (changed by Q2518-Q2524: the port's generator lays a level's starting Mess from its SockAreas when the level starts, on every machine before anything binds, and a Guest's snapshot replaces it with the Host's; no loading screen) **Answer:** Q2518: one `SockArea` box over a room or a floor finds every landing place under it (open floor, tops, insides, under-gaps), tinted by kind; furniture inside it joins automatically.
2. **Do furniture pieces carry their own landing tops?** (a) Yes: each piece's `Spots/Top<n>` (and `Inside`, and the crawl gap) is a landing place with a weight, so a piece dropped in a room brings its places with it. (b) No: only level areas count; the pieces' Spots are ignored. (c) Yes, but each piece's place is off unless an area in the level covers it. Recommended: (a): it is what Q2176 and the old game did (tops, insides and unders per piece), and moving a piece moves its Socks; a level `SockExclusion` still wins. **Answer:** Q2519: furniture brings its own places (`Spots/Top<n>`, `Inside`, the crawl gap); moving a piece moves its Socks.
3. **Counts: per area, per floor, or both.** (a) Each area has its own count. (b) The floor's Resource has one count (Pairs) and each area a weight that shares it out (as the old level shares and top areas). (c) The floor's count with weights, and an area may pin an exact count (a shelf with 3). Recommended: (c): one number to balance per floor, as the old `stages.pairs`, and exact counts where a designer wants a set piece; the generator fills the pinned ones first. (Stages are M4; the generator's `records()` is what a stage's spawn will call) **Answer:** Q2520: one Pair count per area, split by each place's size times a weight per kind set on the area; a piece may override its weight (metadata `sock_weight`); no pinned counts.
4. **Which Designs.** (a) The floor's Resource lists the Collections in play and their shares; areas take from the floor's pool. (b) Each area may also limit or weight Designs (rare ones under the bed). (c) Old rule only: every placed Collection by its share, rarer ones hiding more by "hiding_weight". Recommended: (a) plus the old hiding weight as an area flag "hiding spot" (b-lite): keeps the old "rarer hides more" without per-area Design lists to maintain. **Answer:** Q2521: rarity does not decide hiding; the hidden share (inside and under) is one number on the area, 0.3 by default.
5. **Mates.** The old Mate distance is walked through the house's room graph, which the port lacks. (a) Straight-line distance between the two Socks (min to max by Rarity, as the old numbers). (b) Mates in different areas when possible, no distance. (c) Random (no rule) until the house has rooms. Recommended: (a): the old numbers carry over and the preview can show it; walking distance can replace it once the house has a graph. **Answer:** Q2525: 0.4 to 6 m apart in a straight line; a better distance is core/53.
6. **Preview: dots or Sock shapes.** (a) Dots (small spheres or a point MultiMesh) at each landing point. (b) Small Sock shapes in each Design's colour at the real pose (box-sized, lying, turned). (c) Dots by default, Sock shapes on a toggle. Recommended: (b): the designer judges a Mess by how it looks (overlaps, Heaps, edges), a coloured box per Sock is as cheap as a dot in a MultiMesh, and it shows the turn and the lean. (the port lays a station's 110 Socks in the level's start-up frame; no worker thread or slicing yet: a [later] for a floor of about 1200) **Answer:** Q2522: dots, tinted by kind.
7. **At rest or poured in.** (a) At rest: Socks appear lying where the preview showed (the old game, rows 69 and 73); the Mess gains an `add` of LYING records. (b) Poured in at start through the heap spout's path (they fall and settle physically; the preview shows only where they start). (c) At rest, and an optional per-area "drop from above" for a few. Recommended: (a): the acceptance says the Socks lie where the preview showed, which a physical pour can not promise; the pour stays the zoo's heap station. **Answer:** Q2523: they appear lying where the preview showed, all at once, no fall, no sound; the Mess gains an add of lying records.
8. **Seed per level or per run.** (a) One seed saved on the level (the same Mess every time, authored). (b) A new seed each run, saved with the game (the old New Game, Q839), the level's seed only for the editor and tests. (c) The level's seed by default, a per-run seed as a switch on the floor's Resource. Recommended: (c): the preview needs a fixed seed, play may want variety; the old game varied it every New Game, so the switch keeps that choice open. (not ported: no memo) **Answer:** Q2524: the level's seed by default; a switch for a new seed each New Game, saved with the game.
9. **How the rest is computed.** (a) The old way: a ray down to the surface plus overlap tests against the house and already-placed Socks (no physics step), Heaps by the height-map roll. (b) Simple: a ray down per point, flat on the surface, spacing by a minimum distance between Socks, no stacking (no Heaps). (c) (b) plus the old Heap roll as an area flag "heap". Recommended: (c): one rule works the same in the editor and the game (the editor's own physics space, rays only), Heaps where an area asks for one; the full old shape-cast stacking is the cost listed in Finding 5. (the port keeps the report as `SockGenerator.last_report`: asked, laid, left out, the fails by kind and reason, the hidden Socks moved to open floor; a warning in the log when a Sock finds no place) **Answer:** Q2526: "heap" is an area option (the old roll-down); otherwise flat, spaced, never overlapping. Rest by rays and overlap tests in the editor's and the game's physics space, so the preview and the game agree.
